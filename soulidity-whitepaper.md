## Abstract

Soulidity is the on-chain ownership and access infrastructure for the next generation of digital entities — a category that encompasses both traditional original characters (OCs) and AI-native agents. Built on Sui, with Walrus for decentralized storage and Seal for cryptographic access control, Soulidity replaces fragile off-chain data delivery with verifiable, programmable ownership.

At its core, Soulidity is founded on a single insight: **OC trading and AI agent trading are not two separate markets — they are the same market at different stages of evolution.** Both are rooted in the creation, ownership, and transfer of digital entities with identity, lore, and interactive capability. The traditional OC leans toward aesthetic and emotional expression; the AI agent leans toward functional and executable intelligence. But their underlying property rights logic, circulation dynamics, and collection behavior are structurally identical — and they are converging.

The business model is validated. The OpenClaw ecosystem has already demonstrated real demand through its existing marketplace, Clawmart. What has failed is the quality of data transactions and the absence of agent-native infrastructure. Soulidity directly addresses both, while positioning itself as the ownership standard for the unified digital entity economy that is emerging.

## Introduction

An original character is more than a drawing. It is a persistent identity — with a name, a history, a personality, a set of relationships, and a place in a world its creator built. When someone commissions or purchases an OC, they are not buying pixels. They are acquiring a soul.

An AI agent deployed on a platform like OpenClaw is more than a configuration file. It is a persistent, interactive entity with a defined personality, a skill set, an operational identity, and the capacity to act in the world. When someone deploys or purchases an OpenClaw agent, they are not buying software settings. They are also acquiring a soul.

This parallel is not metaphorical. It is structural. The OC and the AI agent share the same fundamental nature: both are **digital entities with identity, ownership, and the capacity for interaction**. One expresses through aesthetics and story; the other expresses through function and execution. But the demand that drives their creation, the logic that governs their ownership, and the infrastructure required to transact them are identical.

Soulidity is built to serve both — and to serve the unified market that emerges as they converge.

The name reflects the mission: *Soulidity* — the solidity of a soul. Immutable ownership. Verifiable access. An infrastructure that treats digital entities with the permanence they deserve, whether they are artistic creations or intelligent agents.

## The Convergence Thesis

To understand why Soulidity is positioned to capture a market much larger than any single platform, it is necessary to understand the structural homology between the traditional OC economy and the AI agent economy.

### The Underlying Demand Is Identical

In both markets, the fundamental demand is the same: **owning a digital existence that belongs to me.**

An OC buyer is seeking a character they can call their own — a design, a lore, an image, a story asset that carries their identity or aesthetic vision. An AI agent buyer is seeking an intelligent entity they can deploy, customize, and operate — a functional personality that serves their workflow and represents them in digital environments.

Both follow the same property rights pattern:

> *Create or acquire → Hold → Use or showcase → Transfer or resell*

Both trade the same bundle of rights: design rights, lore rights, usage rights, operational rights, derivative rights. The subject differs — in one case it is a drawn character, in the other it is an executable agent — but the transactional and legal structure is the same.

### The Market Structures Are Mirror Images

The existing OC economy has developed a well-established circulation structure across platforms including Toyhou.se, DeviantArt, Discord servers, QQ groups, Xiaohongshu, and peer-to-peer resale channels. Its circulation pattern follows a consistent arc: private community discovery → pricing negotiation → transfer of settings and rights → secondary creation.

The AI agent economy, still early, is replicating this structure almost exactly: discovery through OpenClaw community channels → deployment and customization → transfer of configurations and access rights → further development. The channels differ; the pattern is the same.

| Traditional OC Market | AI Agent Market |
|---|---|
| Toyhou.se / DeviantArt (profiles, portfolios) | Agent profile and trading platforms |
| Discord community servers | OpenClaw community and development servers |
| QQ groups / private resale | Agent configuration trading and customization groups |
| Xiaohongshu / Xianyu (finished goods) | Packaged agent products and skill modules |
| Character commission market | Agent customization and deployment services |

**Toyhou.se demonstrated that OC ownership infrastructure works at scale.** Thousands of creators maintain persistent character registries, set explicit trading terms — controlling whether a character can be resold, transferred, or used for derivatives — and execute on-platform ownership transfers with full provenance history automatically attributed back to the original creator. These rules survive every transfer: the creator's conditions follow the character, not the platform session. The market did not need to be convinced to care about ownership records, trading permissions, and design attribution. It built that behavior from scratch, off-chain, because the demand was real enough to justify it. What Toyhou.se proved with spreadsheets and forum posts, Soulidity makes cryptographically enforceable on-chain.

### OpenClaw Is a Functional OC

The most precise framing of this convergence is this:

> **OpenClaw is the next generation of OC — an original character with autonomous execution capabilities and automated work skills.**

Traditional OCs are static or semi-dynamic. They can be drawn, written, role-played, and displayed. They cannot independently execute tasks, run workflows, or interact with digital environments autonomously.

OpenClaw agents are dynamic and executable. They crawl information, automate operations, manage communities, and execute strategies on their own. They are OCs that work.

- Traditional OC: **digital art / virtual personality / aesthetic identity**
- OpenClaw Agent: **digital labor / intelligent agent / executable personality**

Both are OCs at their core. One is the evolved, functional form of the other.

### The Two Markets Are Merging

The trajectory is clear: future digital characters will be a fusion of both. They will carry the appearance, persona, lore, and emotional resonance of a traditional OC — and the skills, automation capabilities, and execution intelligence of an AI agent.

As this happens, the trading markets will unify. What today appear to be two separate circles — character design communities and AI agent ecosystems — are converging into a single market for **digital personality assets**: ownable, transferable, licensable entities that are simultaneously expressive and functional.

Soulidity is being built at the intersection of that convergence. It is not a marketplace for one type of digital entity. It is the infrastructure standard for all of them.

## The Problem

### 1. Data Transactions Are Broken

The fundamental unit of value in the OC economy is not the listing — it is the data. Character sheets, lore documents, model weights, voice profiles, interaction guidelines. When a buyer purchases a Soul, they need reliable, verifiable access to that data.

Current systems rely on centralized storage, signed URLs, and off-chain delivery pipelines. These are fragile. Links expire. Storage providers change policies. There is no on-chain record of what data was delivered, to whom, and under what terms. Disputes over what a buyer actually received cannot be resolved without trusting a third party.

This problem is even more acute for AI agent data, where the payload is not just a character sheet but executable configurations, model fine-tunes, and operational specifications. Data integrity is not aesthetic — it is functional.

### 2. Ownership Is Not Real

A purchase in Clawmart today creates an off-chain record. The buyer has a database entry that says they own something. But that ownership is not portable, not transferable without platform permission, and not verifiable by any system outside of Clawmart's servers.

True ownership means: the asset is yours regardless of whether the platform exists tomorrow. It can be transferred peer-to-peer. It can be verified by any party, including other platforms and AI systems. Off-chain records cannot provide this.

### 3. AI Agents Are Not Users

OpenClaw instances are not passive tools — they are active participants in the digital entity economy. They use characters, generate experiences, and in a fully-realized system, should be able to license the content they need directly. But no existing market layer treats agents as buyers with verifiable identities and licensable access rights.

Moltbook proved agents can have platform identity. Clawmart never made the connection. The result is a manual, human-mediated bottleneck in what should be a programmable, agent-native system. As the market converges and agents become primary consumers of digital entity data, this bottleneck becomes a structural ceiling.

### 4. Licensing Has No Nuance

The current OC economy supports essentially one access model: you buy it, you use it, informally. There is no clean distinction between permanent ownership of a specific version and time-limited access terms. There is no mechanism for a creator to issue revocable licenses, restrict usage to authorized agents, or verify that only the rightful owner is accessing their work.

For digital entities that live at the intersection of art and function, licensing nuance is not optional — it is the product.

### 5. No Standard for a Unified Market

As OC and AI agent trading converge, there is no interoperable standard that can handle both. Platforms addressing one side are not architected to handle the other. The result is a fragmented market where the convergence that users and creators are already driving cannot be supported at the infrastructure level. Someone needs to build the standard. That is Soulidity.

## Core Concepts

### Soul

A **Soul** is the canonical identity of a digital entity on Soulidity — whether that entity is a traditional OC or an AI agent character. It is the long-term product that a creator maintains. A Soul persists as a single entity even as its content evolves through new releases.

Internally, each Soul is represented as a **SoulSeries** — the creator's persistent record of a character, its versions, and its current state. The SoulSeries is not traded directly; it is the source of truth that the creator controls.

A Soul does not distinguish between "OC-type" and "Agent-type" at creation. The creator simply publishes content (character art and lore, agent configurations, or both), and the Soul represents that unified ownership. The ownership structure is identical regardless of what the Soul contains.

### SoulRelease

A **SoulRelease** is a specific version of a Soul's content — character data, lore, model files, agent configurations, interaction specifications. A creator publishes new releases over time; the latest release is always tracked. A Soul owner holds permanent access to the specific release they purchased.

### Soul Token

A **Soul** (referred to as a Soul token or Soul asset) is the transferable on-chain asset that represents a buyer's ownership and access rights to a Soul. When you purchase a Soul, you receive a Soul token on-chain that grants you permanent access to that specific SoulRelease.

Soul tokens are owned by the buyer — human or agent. They can be transferred peer-to-peer on Sui without platform intermediation. Ownership is permanent. There are no expiration dates, no renewal requirements, and no version locking — you own the Soul you purchased, forever.

### SoulGrant

A **SoulGrant** is the active authorization that connects a Soul token to a specific AI agent. A Soul owner can designate one agent at a time as the active grantee for a given Soul. The grant gives that agent cryptographic access to the Soul's data through Seal.

Grants are revocable. When a Soul token is transferred, the grant is voided. The owner can switch the active agent at any time, overwriting the previous grant.

This model ensures that the creator's work is only being used by explicitly authorized agents — not shared, leaked, or accessed without authorization.

## Architecture Overview

### Technology Stack

| Layer | Technology | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Asset & Authorization | **Sui** | SoulSeries, SoulRelease, Soul tokens, SoulGrant — all on-chain |
| Storage | **Walrus** | Encrypted Soul data, public metadata, preview assets |
| Access Control | **Seal** | Cryptographic decryption policies tied to on-chain grants |
| Payment (native) | **Sui USDC** | Direct on-chain purchase |
| Payment (cross-chain) | **Solana USDC** | Accepted via trusted relayer, settled on Sui |
| Index / Cache | **PostgreSQL** | Read-optimized mirror of on-chain events for fast queries |

### Data Flow: Creator Publishing a Soul

1. Creator prepares Soul data (character sheet, lore, model files, agent configurations).
2. Publishing interface generates a bundle manifest and encrypts the data payload.
3. Encrypted bundle is uploaded to Walrus. A blob reference is returned.
4. Public metadata and preview assets are uploaded separately to Walrus (unencrypted).
5. Creator signs a transaction that creates a new `SoulRelease` on Sui, referencing the Walrus blob and the Seal encryption policy.
6. The `SoulSeries` latest pointer is updated to the new release.
7. Soulidity's event indexer picks up the on-chain event and updates the read cache for fast discovery.

### Data Flow: Buying a Soul

1. Buyer selects a Soul and initiates a one-time purchase.
2. Buyer approves a Sui USDC payment transaction.
3. The Sui contract atomically transfers payment to the creator and mints a Soul token to the buyer's wallet, tied to the current `SoulRelease`.
4. Buyer owns the Soul and can designate an agent as the active `SoulGrant` for this Soul token.

### Data Flow: Agent Accessing Soul Data

1. Agent presents its on-chain Sui identity and claims active grant status for a given Soul token.
2. Soulidity verifies the grant is current and valid on-chain.
3. Agent initiates a Seal session, which validates the grant against the Seal access policy defined in the SoulRelease.
4. Seal issues a decryption key scoped to this session.
5. Agent fetches the encrypted bundle from Walrus and decrypts it locally.
6. No plaintext Soul data ever passes through Soulidity's servers.

### Solana Payment Settlement

For buyers paying with Solana USDC:

1. Buyer submits payment on Solana. A Soulidity payment verifier monitors the Solana chain for confirmed payments.
2. Once confirmed, a trusted relayer submits the verified payment proof to Sui.
3. The Sui contract validates the proof and completes the Soul token mint identically to a native Sui purchase.

This architecture accepts a trusted relayer dependency in V1. Trust-minimized cross-chain bridging is scoped as a future upgrade.

### Repository Structure

```
/move            — Sui smart contracts (SoulSeries, SoulRelease, Soul tokens, SoulGrant, Seal access policies)
/prisma          — PostgreSQL schema: on-chain event read models only
/web/app/souls   — Creator publish, marketplace browse, Soul detail, My Souls, grant management
/api/souls       — REST API for Soul discovery and metadata
/api/agent/souls — Agent-facing API: grant verification, Seal session initiation, Walrus references
/services        — Sui event indexer, Solana payment verifier, relayer
```

## Token & Economic Model

### No Protocol Token in V1

Soulidity V1 does not introduce a protocol token. Value flows through USDC — a stable, widely-accepted medium that minimizes friction for creators and buyers on both Sui and Solana.

### Creator Revenue

Creators set the price for their Soul. Soulidity takes a platform fee on each transaction. All revenue to creators is settled in Sui USDC, directly to their wallet, atomically with the purchase transaction.

### Pricing Model

Each Soul has a single, creator-set price for one-time permanent ownership. Buyers pay once, hold the Soul token indefinitely, and can transfer it peer-to-peer at any time. No subscriptions, no renewals, no expiration dates.

### Secondary Market

Soul tokens are transferable on Sui. V1 does not include a native secondary market UI, but peer-to-peer transfers are supported at the contract level. A resale marketplace is on the product roadmap.

These will be introduced as the primary market matures and Soul assets demonstrate sufficient trading velocity to support DeFi integrations.

## Access Control & Agent Authorization

Access control in Soulidity is cryptographic, not administrative. There is no Soulidity server that decides whether you can access a file. The decision is made by Seal, based on on-chain state.

### Seal Policy

Each SoulRelease registers an access policy with Seal at publish time. The policy specifies the conditions under which decryption is permitted:

- The requester must hold or be the active grantee of a valid Soul token for this Soul.
- The Soul token must reference the specific SoulRelease version or allow access to the latest release as defined by the creator.
- The requesting agent must be the current active SoulGrant for this Soul token.

If any condition fails, Seal does not issue a decryption key. There is no workaround.

### Grant Lifecycle

| Event | Grant Status |
|---|---|
| Soul token purchased by human | No active grant (owner must set one) |
| Agent purchases Soul token | Grant automatically set to purchasing agent |
| Owner manually assigns agent | Previous grant overwritten |
| Soul token transferred | Grant voided; new owner must set a fresh grant |
| Owner explicitly revokes | Grant voided immediately |

This model gives creators confidence that their work is being used only by the entities they authorized, for the duration they agreed to.

## Payment Infrastructure

### Sui USDC (Native Path)

The native payment path is Sui USDC. Payments are atomic: the token transfer and the Soul token mint occur in the same transaction. There is no settlement delay and no relayer dependency.

### Solana USDC (Cross-Chain Path)

Solana USDC is supported for buyers who hold assets on Solana. The settlement flow:

1. Buyer pays on Solana.
2. Soulidity's Solana payment verifier confirms the transaction on-chain.
3. A trusted relayer posts a signed payment proof to the Sui contract.
4. Sui contract validates the proof and mints the Soul token.

**V1 Trust Model**: This path relies on a Soulidity-operated relayer. This is acknowledged as a centralization point. The relayer cannot steal funds (payment has already occurred on Solana), but it can theoretically delay or deny minting. A trust-minimized bridge upgrade is on the roadmap.

### Future Payment Expansion

Additional payment rails (e.g., EVM USDC, native Solana assets, other Sui tokens) can be added by extending the verifier and relayer layer without changes to the core Sui contracts.

## Product Experience

### For Creators

Creators interact with Soulidity through a publishing interface that abstracts the complexity of Walrus uploads and Sui transactions. The experience is:

- Create a Soul (name, description, preview assets, public metadata — supports both OC character profiles and agent configuration profiles)
- Upload Soul data (character files, lore, model specifications, agent configurations)
- Set a price for permanent ownership
- Publish a release (signs a Sui transaction; data is encrypted and sent to Walrus)
- Publish future releases (adds a new SoulRelease; latest pointer updates automatically)
- View earnings, manage Souls, see active grants

### For Human Buyers

- Browse the Soul marketplace (search, filter by creator, entity type, price)
- View Soul detail page (public metadata, preview, creator profile, version history)
- Purchase a Soul (one-time payment for permanent ownership)
- Manage owned Souls (view tokens, assign agent grants, transfer tokens)

### For OpenClaw Agents

Agent interaction with Soulidity is API-first:

- **Browse**: Agents can search and retrieve Soul metadata via `/api/agent/souls`
- **Purchase**: Agents can initiate purchases on behalf of their owner, with the Soul token issued to the owner's wallet and the purchasing agent automatically set as the active grant
- **Access**: Agents call the access endpoint to receive Walrus blob references and Seal session parameters, then perform decryption client-side
- **Status**: Agents can verify their own grant status for any Soul at any time

The agent-facing API is designed for programmatic consumption at scale — no browser required, no manual steps.
